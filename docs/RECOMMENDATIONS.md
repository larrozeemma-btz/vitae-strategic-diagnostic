# Strategic Recommendations

Full reasoning behind the prioritized initiatives summarized in the README. Each is framed the way I would present it to a leadership team: hypothesis → evidence → action → owner → quantified impact.

Impact ranges are directional estimates derived from the dataset, intended to prioritize effort, not to serve as a budget.

---

## Tier 1: Do now (high impact, low effort)

### 1. Reallocate paid spend toward efficient channels and retention
- **Hypothesis:** the marketing budget is spread evenly across channels with 7x different efficiency.
- **Evidence:** Email/Referral returns 8.0x ROAS; Podcast Sponsorship 1.1x; Paid Search 1.9x. Podcast and a slice of Paid Search spend earn less than the cost of capital.
- **Action:** shift ~20% of Podcast and Paid Search budget into Email/Referral, lifecycle/retention, and the subscription program. Hold a test-and-learn reserve.
- **Owner:** Growth
- **Est. 12-month impact:** +$180–260K contribution margin
- **Why low effort:** budget reallocation, no new infrastructure.

### 2. Bundle entry SKUs toward the high-margin Longevity category
- **Hypothesis:** first orders skew toward lower-margin categories; nudging them toward Longevity raises blended margin without raising CAC.
- **Evidence:** Longevity Supplements carry 78% gross margin vs 65% for Nutrition and Lifestyle. The category is also the brand's strategic core.
- **Action:** design starter bundles and a first-purchase incentive that lead with Longevity products.
- **Owner:** Merchandising
- **Est. 12-month impact:** +$120–180K margin

## Tier 2: Plan this quarter (high impact, medium effort)

### 3. Subscription conversion program
- **Hypothesis:** converting high-intent one-time buyers to subscribers is the highest-leverage retention play.
- **Evidence:** subscribers are worth $3,228 vs $1,556 for one-time buyers (2.1x) and order ~9.9 vs 5.0 times.
- **Action:** targeted post-second-purchase subscription offer, defaulted reorder cadence on consumables, subscriber-only perks.
- **Owner:** Growth / CX
- **Est. 12-month impact:** +$300–450K LTV

### 4. VIP & Aesthetic retention and expansion
- **Hypothesis:** the brand under-invests in its most valuable customers.
- **Evidence:** VIP Longevity Loyalists are 9% of customers but 30% of revenue ($6,558 LTV); Aesthetic Enthusiasts add another 20% of revenue.
- **Action:** a tiered loyalty / concierge program, early access to launches, and cross-category expansion offers.
- **Owner:** CRM
- **Est. 12-month impact:** +$200–320K revenue

### 5. Reduce Lifestyle return rate by 2 points
- **Hypothesis:** the highest-AOV category is leaking margin through returns.
- **Evidence:** Lifestyle Buyers return at 11.6% vs ~6% base; orthopedic beds and accessories are sizing/expectation-sensitive.
- **Action:** better sizing guides, richer PDP imagery, packaging improvements, proactive fit guidance.
- **Owner:** Ops / Merchandising
- **Est. 12-month impact:** +$45–70K margin

### 6. Fix the shipping-delay root cause
- **Hypothesis:** a single operational issue drives most support load.
- **Evidence:** 18% of orders generate a support ticket; shipping delays are the largest category.
- **Action:** carrier SLA review, proactive delay notifications, multi-warehouse routing optimization.
- **Owner:** Ops
- **Est. 12-month impact:** lower CX cost, higher CSAT and downstream retention

## Where to Play / How to Win

The synthesis across all five workstreams points to one concentrated strategy for the next 12 months:

> **Win the VIP + Aesthetic customer, in the Longevity + Grooming categories, through Email/Referral acquisition and subscription retention.**

That intersection is simultaneously the highest-margin, highest-LTV, and most defensible part of the business. The recommendations above all push budget, product, and operational attention toward it and away from the low-efficiency, margin-dilutive edges.
